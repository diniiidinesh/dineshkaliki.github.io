Timeline Missions: Moving Retention Across Seven Games

Leading an internal gamification and customer data platform serving 100mn+ users, and shipping the feature that moved retention, player value, and revenue — without breaking the games it touched.

Role: Product Manager I Company: Junglee Games (Flutter) Duration: 18 months, Apr 2022 – Nov 2023 Tools: Internal CDP/Audience platform, in-house A/B testing framework

The platform

I owned Audience, Junglee Games' internal gamification and customer data platform — serving 100mn+ gamers across seven games, powering 2,000+ campaigns and funnels a month, with a team of 4 developers and 2 associate product managers. The platform replaced a third-party tool (CleverTap) after I built the roadmap and business case for an in-house replacement, saving ₹2 Crores annuallyHard while giving us an extensible architecture built for the platform's own needs rather than a vendor's.

The problem

Timeline Missions started as a problem, not a feature request: the Rummy product marketing manager flagged that engagement wasn't sticky enough across sessions, and there was no gamification mechanic giving users a reason to come back day after day, in a way that scaled across games with very different economics.

Approach

Rather than building the full system speculatively, I built a proof of concept on top of an existing system — Adaptive Missions — to de-risk the idea before committing engineering time to a ground-up build. The design used day-gated progression to build a habit loop. The trickiest part wasn't the mechanic itself but a revenue-leakage risk it created: a user active in one mission shouldn't be able to enter a second, overlapping mission, or the platform would effectively be paying out twice for the same engagement. I designed the eligibility logic explicitly around that constraint, and because the feature touched both Howzat and Rummy — two client teams with different stakes — I took it through sign-off from both before shipping, rather than shipping to one and retrofitting the other.

Outcome

Timeline Missions elevated Retention up to 20%, Player Value up to 17.2%, and Net Gaming Revenue up to 21.4% across gamesHard. The more meaningful numbers are cohort-specific rather than a single blended figure:

  • 19.8% D14 retention lift for new users, 21.4% D7 NGR/user for existing users, on Poker
  • 8.9% D21 retention growth, 17.2% D21 player-value increase, 3% D21 NGR rise, for Rummy on Howzat
  • 3% D14 retention uplift and 1.2% D14 player-value uplift for Fantasy Quests on Howzat

What I'd flag in an interview

The headline "up to 20%" number is real, but I'd rather lead with the cohort breakdown in a conversation — it's the more honest and more useful version of the result, and it's what let us understand why new and existing users responded differently. The de-risking step (building on Adaptive Missions rather than from scratch) and the cross-team sign-off process are also worth more than they look on a resume line: they're the difference between a feature that ships once and a feature that ships without a rollback three weeks later.
